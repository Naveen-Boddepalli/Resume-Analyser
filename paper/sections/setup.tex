\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\subsection{Data}
We use the public Hugging Face benchmark \texttt{cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit}: 6{,}241
training and 1{,}759 test pairs with labels No Fit (50\%), Potential Fit (25\%) and Good Fit (25\%). The
hub page publishes no dataset card or licence, and how the labels were produced is undocumented; we treat the
benchmark as research data of uncertain provenance and verify file checksums before every run. The
resumes in this data set are already anonymised in places (placeholder city and state), so the cost of
redaction measured here is a lower bound for raw resumes.

\subsection{Splits}
\textbf{S1} is the official split. It is job-description-disjoint, but 99.9\% of test resumes also occur in
training. \textbf{S2} pools all 8{,}000 pairs, partitions resumes and job descriptions at random into five
folds each (seed 13), and for fold $k$ trains on pairs whose resume \emph{and} job description fall outside
fold $k$ and tests on pairs whose resume \emph{and} job description fall inside it. No test resume or posting is
seen in training, and the five test sets are disjoint. Because both axes are held out, S2 has fewer
candidates per posting than S1, so its ranking metrics are not comparable in level with S1.

\subsection{Systems compared}
\textbf{B0} predicts the majority class. \textbf{B0r} predicts the training label majority of the
\emph{resume} and is a leakage diagnostic, not a competitor. \textbf{B1--B4} score each pair with TF-IDF
cosine, BM25, MiniLM \cite{reimers2019sbert,wang2020minilm} document cosine or BGE-small document cosine
\cite{xiao2024cpack} on raw text and learn only two score thresholds on training data. \textbf{B6} trains
logistic regression on the concatenation $[\mathbf u\odot\mathbf v,|\mathbf u-\mathbf v|,\cos]$ of whole-document MiniLM
embeddings. \textbf{M1} uses lexical, skill and experience features; \textbf{M2} only semantic features;
\textbf{M3} is EviMatch with a linear head and \textbf{M4} with a gradient-boosted head. \textbf{X1/X2} add the
excluded single-document features to M3/M4 to measure leakage.

\subsection{Training and model selection}
Hyper-parameters are chosen by five-fold grouped cross-validation on the training portion only, with
folds grouped by job description. The grid is $C\in\{0.003,\dots,1\}$ for the linear head and depth
$\{2,3\}\times$ iterations $\{100,200\}$ for the boosted head (learning rate 0.05). Under the
one-standard-error rule we take the simplest candidate whose mean macro-F1 is within one standard error of
the best. The sentence encoder is \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} (22.7M parameters) with a 128-token limit per sentence
unit. Experiments ran on an Apple M4 laptop (16 GB) in a few minutes per configuration; all seeds are 13 and
library versions and the git commit are stored with every result file.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The primary metric is macro-F1 over the three classes. We also report accuracy, one-vs-rest ROC-AUC for
Good Fit, per-class precision/recall/F1, a confusion matrix, quadratic-weighted kappa, and for ranking
nDCG@10 per posting (graded gains 0/1/2, postings with at least five candidates, seeded random
tie-breaking). Intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals that resample \emph{job descriptions},
the unit of independence; differences between systems use paired resamples.
The evaluation protocol, including every deviation made after it was written, is in the repository.
